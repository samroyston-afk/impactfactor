Our Mission
The Idea

Good research does not travel by itself

The UK produces an enormous volume of high quality research on the questions that matter most in social policy, health, welfare and public services. Only a fraction of it changes what anyone does. Meanwhile, decisions get taken in those same fields on the basis of assumption, anecdote and whatever evidence happened to be within reach.

That gap is not caused by lack of research or weak evidence. It is caused by the way impact is usually approached.

01

Impact is considered too late to be impactful

It is treated as a reporting exercise at the end of a project, when the findings are already fixed and the audience has moved on. By the time anyone asks who needs to hear this, the moment to influence has usually passed.

02

The project is mistaken for the asset

Impact planning is done project by project, so it restarts from nothing each time. The thing that actually carries influence is the researcher's accumulated expertise, credibility and relationships, which no single project fully captures.

03

The audience is assumed rather than known

Findings are written for other researchers and then translated outwards, on the hope that the right people will find them. Policy and practice audiences work to different questions, timescales and formats, and rarely go looking.

The Approach

Working the problem from the audience side

Impact Factor starts from the change you want to see, and works back to what the research needs to do to bring it about. That means identifying who can act, what they need in order to act, and when they will be making the decision, then building the work and the evidence trail to fit.

It treats a researcher's expertise, skills and networks as the primary asset, and individual projects as the vehicles through which that expertise reaches the world. Done that way, impact compounds across a career instead of resetting with each grant.

And it takes seriously that most impact support comes from inside the academy. The judgements that matter here are about what makes evidence usable to a policy or practice audience, and what makes it easy to set aside.

And yes, the name is deliberate. The real impact factor is not the one in the journal rankings.

Services

Three ways Impact Factor can help

Tailored to where you sit in the research system, whether you are producing research, managing it across an institution, or funding it.

01

For researchers

Support across a full project lifecycle, from first idea through to dissemination after completion.

Before research begins. Help with the impact sections of grant applications, stakeholder mapping, and identifying realistic routes to influence.

During the project. Acting as an ongoing impact partner as the work develops and opportunities appear.

After completion. Research translation events, and support using findings to influence policy and practice audiences.

Working with researchers to understand the long term outcomes they are seeking to deliver beyond any individual project, and the skills, expertise and experiences they can bring to bear to deliver them.

02

For research impact managers

Extra capacity and outside challenge, so REF impact case studies reach their potential and researchers build impact capability.

Impact training for academics. Workshops that build researchers' confidence and capability, including the Impact Ready full-day programme.

Case study drafting. Hands-on support for academics writing REF impact case studies.

External challenge panel. Independent review of draft case studies, with recommendations to strengthen reach and significance.

Impact maximisation. Where review identifies further opportunity, working with researchers to build additional impact before submission.

03

For research funders

Designing and running grant programmes with impact built in from the start, through to evaluation and learning.

Research questions and programme design. Identifying questions with a clear route to real-world change, and designing programmes around them.

Impact review panels. Independent assessment of applications for impact potential.

Impact evaluation. Assessing what completed projects actually changed, and why.

Portfolio learning and dissemination. Drawing together findings across a funding programme and getting them to the audiences that matter.

Support for grant holders. Partnership support to help funded teams maximise the impact of their projects.

Flagship Programme

Impact Ready

A full-day workshop for researchers, built on a simple reframe. Your expertise, skills and networks are your primary impact asset. Individual projects are the vehicles through which that expertise reaches the world.

Chapter One

What impact do you want to have?

Starting from the change you want to see in the world, rather than from the project you happen to be running.

Chapter Two

How will you make a difference?

Mapping the people and organisations who can act on your work, and the routes by which your expertise reaches them.

Chapter Three

How will you grow and scale change?

Building partnerships, momentum and evidence, so early influence turns into lasting change.

How the day works

  • Full day, 09:00 to 15:30, delivered on campus
  • Short pre-work primes each of the three chapters
  • Practical exercises rather than lecture: expertise audits, a platform canvas, a stakeholder web and partnership scenarios
  • Tailored to the institution and the disciplines in the room

What participants leave with

  • A completed Impact Plan, built up one section per chapter through the day
  • A clear view of their own expertise, networks and reach
  • A prioritised set of stakeholders and next steps
  • A resource pack, and a follow-up prompt six weeks later to keep the plan alive
